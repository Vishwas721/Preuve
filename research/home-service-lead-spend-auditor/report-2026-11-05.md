# Lead-spend auditor for US home-service contractors: research report (2026-11-05)

Idea: `dbd454e2-9776-492b-baa5-9f562858e0f4` · Method v3 · 82 evidence items (all 82 verified on import)

## Bottom line

The problem is real. Pros on Thumbtack, Angi and HomeAdvisor say they pay for shared, fake and unreachable leads, that refunds are hard to get, and that they can't easily see return by source. Individual pros report spending $10k to $23k+ a year on these leads. The **refund-recovery wedge is weaker than the idea assumes**, though:
- Both Angi and Thumbtack explicitly refuse to refund the most common kind of "waste": customers who don't respond, are just shopping, or hire someone else.
- Platforms are starting to automate credits themselves.
- Housecall Pro already reports cost per job by lead source, including Thumbtack and Angi.

The evidence is mostly complaints from forums, and I found nothing that measures how much money is actually claimable per contractor.

## What supports the problem

- **Shared leads are built into how the platforms work.**
  - Angi may match one request with up to five pros [ev-55].
  - A window cleaner says Angi leads went to 3-4 competitors [ev-35].
  - A Thumbtack handyman paid $83.25 for a lead that two other pros also bought [ev-20].
  - Pros complain that one request is charged to several pros [ev-25].
- **Fake and bad leads.**
  - One pro got a burst of bot leads: 30+ identical leads in 10-15 minutes, about $1,000 of spend, and the refund requests were initially denied [ev-01, ev-02].
  - A handyman blogger reports "dozens of bad leads" [ev-33].
  - The FTC found that HomeAdvisor overstated how often its leads became jobs, and that many leads were for services the pro didn't offer or were out of area [ev-26, ev-27].
- **Refunds are a grind.** Pros describe refunds as "sparingly" granted, "exhausting" to chase, and denied even when they followed the rules [ev-12, ev-13, ev-23]. A Milwaukee tile/bath contractor left Angi and HomeGuide partly because he lacked the time or resources to argue for refunds [ev-15]. An HVAC contractor quit HomeAdvisor after 3 years of fighting credit denials [ev-28].
- **Spend is large and visibility is poor.**
  - Reported spend: over $10k a year [ev-56], and $23k in part of one year [ev-11].
  - Lead cost has reached 45-50% of the revenue it brings in [ev-17, ev-19].
  - A Thumbtack Top Pro says lead-to-hire and ROI ratios are "not something I can get from Thumbtack or online", and that the numbers only show up after months [ev-16, ev-18].
  - Marketplace leads are hard to attribute to a source [ev-45].
- **Platforms tighten their refund rules.**
  - Thumbtack dropped an older 48-hour refund rule [ev-06].
  - Google dropped credits for leads with the wrong job type or location [ev-50].
  - Manual Google disputes had been worth "hundreds of dollars a month" to large accounts [ev-51].

## What argues against it

- **Most waste can't be refunded.** Angi says homeowners who don't answer, are shopping, or chose another pro are "accounted for in our lead pricing" and are not eligible for credits [ev-08]. Thumbtack staff say customers who don't respond are not refundable [ev-05]. What is left to claim is a narrower set: invalid contact details, duplicates, wrong location, service mismatch [ev-10]. Credits come back as account credit, not cash, and Angi's expire after six months [ev-09].
- **Platforms are automating credits themselves.**
  - Google Local Services Ads now credits low-quality leads automatically [ev-49].
  - Thumbtack reportedly auto-refunds if the customer replies to no pro within 72 hours [ev-52].
  - Thumbtack also runs a separate spam-refund process that refunded most of the bot-lead burst above [ev-03, ev-04].
- **Existing tools already cover part of the analytics.** Housecall Pro's ROI table shows results per lead source, including Thumbtack and Angi, with cost per job and cost per scheduled job [ev-38, ev-40]. The gap is that spend has to be entered by hand and lead-source fields are often left blank [ev-39].
- **Some pros are happy with the ROI.** A handyman reports about 3.8x on HomeAdvisor in year one, and knew that number without any special tool [ev-32]. A window cleaner tripled his money on Angi [ev-36]. A Thumbtack pro calls it "a good ROI" [ev-22]. Even a critical agency says handymen do well on HomeAdvisor [ev-30]. Some complaints are blamed on contractors who can't close [ev-34].
- **The platform may change its pricing.** Angi has said pros could eventually be charged per appointment or per job won [ev-53]. That would shrink the paid-for-a-bad-lead problem.

## How it's solved today

- **Indirect:**
  - Housecall Pro Marketing ROI table [ev-38 to ev-40]
  - Jobber lead-source field [ev-41, ev-42]
  - SourceLoop attribution, which covers ads and calls but not marketplace refunds [ev-47]
  - The platforms' own credit flows: Angi [ev-07 to ev-10], Thumbtack and its Fair Shot refund [ev-04, ev-52], and Google LSA auto-credits [ev-49 to ev-51]
- **Manual:**
  - Spreadsheets and owner math [ev-14, ev-19, ev-46]
  - Workiz's free tracking template [ev-43, ev-44]
  - A DIY dashboard built in RationalGo [ev-48]
- **Service:** Contractor marketing agencies that pitch owned channels instead of shared leads [ev-29, ev-31].
- **Direct, not verified:** **Tack Tools Pro**, a Thumbtack lead-response tool. Search snippets say it added a "Fair Credit" feature in 2026 that drafts refund requests, starting at $99 a month. Its press release and G2 page could not be fetched, so it is not cited in the pack. It looks like the closest direct competitor to the refund wedge and should be checked by hand.
- The founder listed no competitors.

## Prospects (primary ICP: solo/micro home-service pros)

I found 19 companies, one short of the 20 target. All come from the companies' own websites, because Thumbtack, Angi and HomeAdvisor profile pages can't be fetched.
- **11 show a Thumbtack or Angi signal:** Julia Clean Pro, Sandia Green Clean, Valley's Best Cleaners, Handyman Rescue Team, Upkeep Lawn Care, Discard Junk Removal, ProDry Floor Care, West Coast Power-Vac, Handy Men & Mrs. Helper, Turn2Us, Shark Tile.
- **Upkeep Lawn Care publicly stopped using Thumbtack [ev-63].** It is a good candidate for a lapsed-user interview.
- **The rest are owner-operated handyman or tile shops** with no marketplace signal found.
- **Contacts:** 8, all named with a title on the company's own pages. Several are first names only.
- **Size:** Unknown for all 19. No company states a headcount.

## Method notes

- **Queries:** 21 recorded in `meta.queries`. They include contradiction searches on "worth it" / ROI, refunds that were approved, and the platforms' pricing changes.
- **Source mix:** Thumbtack Community forum threads, a window-cleaner forum, Angi and Google help pages, TechCrunch coverage of the FTC case, agency and vendor blogs, and company sites.
- **Limits:**
  - The sources are heavily biased toward complaints.
  - BBB, Trustpilot, FTC.gov, Jobber help, the HomeAdvisor help pages, Alignable, G2 and the Tack Tools Pro release were not fetchable and were left out.
  - The Fair Shot Refund detail comes from a third-party blog that summarizes Thumbtack's help pages, not from Thumbtack directly. It also conflicts with a Thumbtack moderator's statement that customers who don't respond are not refundable [ev-05].
