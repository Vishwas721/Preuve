# Research report: EU unsold-textile destruction-ban compliance and recommerce routing

## Bottom line

The regulation is real and in force. Since 19 July 2026 large companies may not destroy unsold apparel, accessories or footwear, and recycling counts as destruction [ev-01]. Firms must keep five years of delivery and reception evidence, including waste-operator statements [ev-02], and document every derogation [ev-04]. So the compliance-evidence need is genuine.

The commercial case is much weaker than the idea assumes:
- **Little stock was being destroyed.** The Commission estimates only 4–9% of unsold textiles were destroyed [ev-07]. Industry associations say surplus already goes to outlets, charity and stock buyers [ev-24][ev-26].
- **Enforcement is soft so far.** French fines cap at EUR 15k, and France had not yet empowered its enforcer as of September 2026 [ev-22]. The mandatory disclosure template only starts with FY2028 for calendar-year companies [ev-20].
- **The exact wedge is already taken.** Musthad already routes excess to 300+ vetted operators, with tracking and reporting [ev-38]. TrusTrace, ReBound and Dcycle sell the disclosure and tracking layer [ev-42][ev-44][ev-43].

The evidence supports a narrow evidence-and-reporting need. It does not show a large, unserved routing market.

## What supports the problem

- **Legal hard date and scope.** The ban has applied since 19 July 2026 and also prohibits recycling unsold goods [ev-01]. Medium-sized companies follow in 2030.
- **Chain-of-custody evidence is mandatory.** Firms need five years of delivery and reception documentation with waste-operator statements [ev-02], plus derogation-specific documentation [ev-04]. Using the donation derogation requires a documented offer to three EU social-economy entities, or eight weeks on the company website [ev-06].
- **Reporting data is fragmented today:**
  - Dispositions are tracked in ERPs, delivery notes and recycler emails [ev-16].
  - Brands depend on waste providers to report each batch's fate [ev-18].
  - ASOS's own ESPR disclosure says it still needs data to verify reseller behaviour and waste routes [ev-30].
  - More than three-quarters of "responsible" brands disclose nothing about unsold goods [ev-57].
- **The sector is described as ill-prepared** because the reuse supply chain is weak [ev-13]. Advisers expect substantial cost and operational challenges for high-return online sellers [ev-09]. About a third of online returns were destined for destruction [ev-14].
- **Organisational shift.** Unsold inventory is becoming a metric for sustainability, finance and compliance teams [ev-32]. H&M created a CSCO role with end-to-end inventory-flow responsibility [ev-34].
- **Cost of holding excess.** Apparel carrying cost is about 20–25% of value per year [ev-35]. A vendor claims savings of 44% on labour, 30% on storage and 18% on disposal (self-reported) [ev-40].
- **A ban shifts volume.** After France's AGEC ban, one donation network's corporate donations rose from 3M to 10M products between 2019 and 2023 [ev-51].

## What argues against it

- **Only 4–9% of unsold textiles were destroyed** [ev-07]. The ban affects a small slice of excess stock.
- **Incumbent channels already absorb surplus.** German and Belgian associations say surplus goes to outlets, charity and stock buyers, and that the law "mainly confirms existing practices" plus record-keeping [ev-23][ev-24][ev-26]. ASOS says destroying stock is not in its interest [ev-31].
- **Weak, uneven enforcement.** French fines cap at EUR 15k and France had no empowered enforcer as of September 2026 [ev-22]. Penalties vary by Member State [ev-10]. The German association calls the ban a "paper tiger" [ev-25]. Many firms are waiting [ev-28].
- **The hard reporting deadline is later.** The mandatory template starts with FY2028 for calendar-year companies [ev-20]. Third-party assurance was dropped [ev-05].
- **Cheap loopholes.** Bulk export to non-EU traders counts as reuse on paper [ev-27].
- **Recovered value is small.** Distressed apparel recovers about 1.4–2% of retail at auction, and donation returns zero cash [ev-36][ev-37]. A 5–15% share of recovered value may not fund the business.
- **Brands want discretion.** Brands avoid appearing on a central list of excess sellers, and many sell under NDA [ev-59][ev-60]. This cuts against a visible marketplace.
- **Partner capacity is constrained:**
  - Donation networks refuse 15–20% of offers [ev-53].
  - Sorters are overloaded [ev-56].
  - EU mechanical recycling covers about 1% of the volume [ev-17].
- **Unsold volume is falling at large donors** because they produce fewer unsold goods [ev-52].
- **Crowded intermediaries.** French unsold-goods brokers already exist and face criticism [ev-54][ev-55].

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:**
  - Musthad: routing to 300+ vetted operators, with tracking and reporting; raised EUR 1M in 2026 [ev-38][ev-41].
  - TrusTrace: software for disposition tracking and ESPR disclosures [ev-42].
- **Indirect:**
  - ReBound: returns routing plus ESPR reporting [ev-44][ev-45].
  - Dcycle: ESRS E5 data platform [ev-43].
  - smatch and Unfrosen: discreet B2B off-price marketplaces [ev-47][ev-48].
  - B-Stock: B2B recommerce [ev-63].
- **Service:**
  - Reflaunt with DHL: resale-as-a-service [ev-46].
  - Comerso: donation logistics with traceability [ev-50].
  - Agence du Don en Nature: donation network [ev-51].
  - French unsold-goods broker start-ups [ev-55].
  - Consultancies such as Anthesis: ESPR gap analyses [ev-61].
- **Manual:** ERP exports, delivery notes and recycler emails, plus amended contracts with disposal providers [ev-16][ev-03].
- **Founder-listed competitors:**
  - Musthad and Reflaunt are confirmed above.
  - Percentil was a consumer second-hand site, not a brand-excess router, and filed for bankruptcy in March 2025 [ev-62].
  - **Recovo**: its site disallows Preuve's crawler, so it was not evidenced.
  - **ZeroW**: no public evidence found.

## Method notes

- **Queries:** 22 searches. These included contradiction searches (brands that say they don't destroy stock, AGEC enforcement) and a complaint search (Otrium, which returned only consumer reviews and was not used).
- **Source mix:** law-firm and adviser briefings (Cooley, CMS, Linklaters, Projet Celsius, Anthesis), EU sources (Commission, EU Textiles Ecosystem Platform), trade news (FashionUnited, Glossy, L'info durable, LaConceria), vendor sites and company pages.
- **Unfetchable sources, not cited:** just-style, Otrium, Recovo, Inspectorio, the Greenpeace and Mammut PDFs, EuroCommerce, LSA, and WSJ via marketscreener.
- **Conflicting secondary sources:** FashionUnited describes destruction as excluding recycling, but the law-firm sources and the BRC say recycling is included. This report follows the law firms.
