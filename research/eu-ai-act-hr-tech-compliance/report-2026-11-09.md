# EU AI Act compliance system for HR-tech vendors and employers: research report (2026-11-09)

Idea: `6bbbd90d-d9b9-4e30-9435-cb865afb8efd` · Method v3 · 67 evidence items (67 verified, 0 unverifiable, 0 rejected) · 11 competitors · 19 prospect companies

## Bottom line

The demand signal is real, but it comes from procurement. EU enterprise buyers are adding AI Act blocks to vendor questionnaires, and HR-tech vendors say customers want independent evidence [ev-10][ev-17][ev-31]. The legal clock is weaker than the idea assumes. Annex III employment obligations now start on 2 December 2027 [ev-01], and products already on the market before then may keep selling without new obligations unless materially changed [ev-02][ev-03]. The HR-specific wedge is also already occupied. FairNow (now part of AuditBoard) and Warden AI sell governance, bias audits and documentation directly to HR-tech vendors [ev-33][ev-34][ev-29]. The evidence supports "vendors need AI Act evidence to close deals". It does not support "there is an empty niche for an employment-specific system of record".

## What supports the problem

- **Buyers ask in procurement.** EU enterprise legal and procurement teams add AI-specific question blocks to vendor assessments [ev-10]. Procurement teams demanding AI Act evidence in tenders is described as the first cost most organisations face [ev-08]. TestGorilla says buyers' legal teams "are already asking these questions" [ev-17].
- **Evidence matters commercially.** PageUp's CEO says independent audits help "move from promises to evidence, which is what enterprise customers are increasingly looking for" [ev-31]. Mid-market AI vendors lose deals by answering questionnaires ad hoc [ev-15], and each questionnaire takes 10–40 hours of combined effort [ev-14].
- **The burden is significant for providers.** Estimates put a provider of high-risk AI at EUR 100k–500k+ and a mid-sized organisation at EUR 25k–100k [ev-05]. HR-tech SaaS providers usually have to write quality-management documentation from scratch [ev-44].
- **Employers are unprepared.** Fewer than 20% of European employers say they are very prepared, with no improvement in a year [ev-21]. 66% of HR departments have no AI guidelines [ev-22], and only 32% have a named governance role [ev-24]. 21 of Germany's 25 largest employers use AI-capable applicant software, but only 4 tell applicants [ev-25].
- **Bias audits are not enough.** An audited vendor says no audit report substitutes for conformity assessment [ev-32]. Vanta refers customers to a specialist for CE marking [ev-39]. This leaves room for something that produces the full technical file.

## What argues against it

- **The deadline moved, and existing products are grandfathered.** The Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744) is in force, and Annex III now applies from 2 Dec 2027 [ev-01]. Under Art. 111(2), systems already on the market can keep being sold without new obligations unless their design changes materially [ev-02]. A former co-negotiator says pre-deadline hiring AI "may remain outside the AI Act indefinitely" [ev-03]. This weakens the near-term urgency for incumbent vendors.
- **Smaller vendors face lighter rules.** SME relief now extends to small mid-caps, with streamlined documentation [ev-04]. A register in existing tooling is often enough for small organisations [ev-07].
- **The HR-specific niche is taken.** FairNow already serves Dayforce, Ashby, Humanly and Plum with risk assessments, bias audits and technical documentation [ev-33], and AuditBoard has acquired it [ev-34]. Warden AI provides shareable bias audits for Beamery, PageUp and Whippy [ev-29][ev-30][ev-31]. Large vendors such as Workday do the work in-house, with ISO 42001 certification and AI Fact Sheets [ev-19].
- **High-risk HR AI use is thinner than assumed.** 60% of HR departments use no AI in talent management, and many have no budget [ev-23]. Only 12% of German firms use algorithms to assess performance [ev-26]. Airbus restricts recruiting AI in Europe to admin uses, and CV filtering is trialled only in Canada and China [ev-47].
- **Crowded pricing.** Horizontal platforms are numerous and mostly quote-only [ev-37]. Entry-level licences run about EUR 10k–20k a year [ev-06], which is consistent with but not above the EUR 500–2,500/month hypothesis.

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:** FairNow (AuditBoard / Optro) [ev-33][ev-34]; Warden AI [ev-29]–[ev-32].
- **Indirect:** Vanta [ev-35][ev-39], OneTrust [ev-36], Modulos [ev-40], Credo AI [ev-41], Holistic AI [ev-42], IBM watsonx.governance from $3,500/mo [ev-38].
- **Manual:** in-house ISO 42001 programmes (Workday, TestGorilla) [ev-19][ev-18]; spreadsheets, Annex IV Word templates and ad hoc questionnaire answers [ev-41][ev-42][ev-43][ev-15].
- **Service:** consultancies and accounting firms offering bespoke or fixed-price readiness work [ev-05][ev-06].
- The founder named OneTrust, Vanta, Credo AI and Holistic AI, and all four have evidence. "Newer EU-native tools" are covered by Modulos. Law-firm packages for HR-tech specifically were searched for, but no fetchable page was found.

## Method notes

- 24 queries. Sources are consultancy and industry publications, vendor sites, news, two surveys (EAPM/CIPD, Greenhouse) and one public directory (UK government AI assurance catalogue).
- Contradiction searches: "wait and see" after the delay, Art. 6(3) exceptions, and limited HR AI adoption.
- Limits: there are almost no first-hand forum or review voices from HR-tech compliance staff, and no AI Act job postings at HR-tech vendors were found.
- Cost figures are estimates.
- Several pages were unfetchable and left out: the Littler survey on allwork.space, techzine, Corporate Compliance Insights, Verdantix and Data Protection Report. The Littler figures are cited via Recruiting News Network instead.
- Many corporate sites (Allianz, SAP, Bayer, Forvia, Heineken, ST) block Preuve's fetcher, which limited the prospect facts.
