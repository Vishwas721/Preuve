## Bottom line

The problem is real and well documented. Hidden business rules in COBOL, RPG and insurance policy systems cause rework, failed migrations and costly reconstruction (one case: $1M) [ev-01, ev-25, ev-28, ev-29].

Two findings weaken this wedge.
- **The primary ICP may not own the code.** Mid-size US banks and credit unions mostly outsource their core to Fiserv, Jack Henry, FIS or CSI. Some contracts give the vendor ownership of the source code [ev-17, ev-18, ev-19, ev-20, ev-21].
- **"Understanding first" is already sold.** It is bundled into AWS Transform (no service fee), IBM watsonx Code Assistant for Z and Google's Mainframe Assessment Tool. Funded startups (Kodesage, Hypercubic) and many services firms also sell it [ev-47, ev-50, ev-52, ev-54, ev-56, ev-58, ev-61].

Mid-size insurers, and banks that run their own IBM i systems, look like a better fit. This research did not test that.

## What supports the problem

- **Reconstruction is expensive.** One client spent $1M to reconstruct COBOL logic. The vendor's estimate was $200K had the legacy staff still been there, and up to 10x more once the knowledge is gone [ev-01, ev-02].
- **Insurance migrations stumble on rules.**
  - A US P&C insurer's earlier modernization failed. Its rules were buried across thousands of artifacts [ev-25, ev-26].
  - One life-insurance line needed 1,500 rules documented, with considerable rework [ev-28].
  - A Wisconsin insurer called replicating rules a primary challenge [ev-29].
  - Undocumented changes surface late in migration reviews [ev-30].
- **Surveys show confidence drops once projects start.** IT leaders "very confident" in their understanding fall from 41% to 28% after kickoff [ev-12]. 81% struggle to hire or keep legacy programmers [ev-14]. More than half want automated BRE, but few have access to it [ev-16].
- **Job postings show the problem.**
  - A mainframe contract role requires tracing undocumented business logic [ev-24].
  - Bangor Savings Bank ($7B, 1,200+ staff) runs SilverLake in-house on IBM i, wants RPG/CL skills and relies on one SME role for core knowledge [ev-22, ev-23].
- **Vendors and a bank executive describe the same pain.** A large-bank IT transformation head fears losing embedded business logic [ev-03]. IBM and AWS describe missing documentation and SME dependency [ev-48, ev-53]. Vendors' claim that undocumented IBM i code is "the default condition" is opinion [ev-33].

## What argues against it

- **The bank may not own the code.**
  - Most banks and credit unions rely on core providers. The Big Three serve more than 70% of banks [ev-17, ev-18].
  - Some contracts give the provider sole ownership of the source code [ev-19].
  - Most new core deals are outsourced, and few institutions move back in-house [ev-20]. The COBOL often sits at the vendor; CSI maintains about 100 legacy COBOL programs [ev-21].
- **Low urgency.**
  - A worried bank leader still calls proactive knowledge transfer "a hard sell" because "We haven't gotten to the point of feeling the pain yet" [ev-04].
  - 70% of surveyed leaders say they are confident they understand their apps [ev-13].
  - Businesses don't want to spend on incremental rewrites [ev-38].
- **Smaller shops replace rather than preserve.** Small and midsize shops tend to migrate off COBOL when they retire hardware [ev-05].
- **Free and bundled competition.**
  - AWS Transform has no service fee for mainframe work, and AWS "monetizes the destination" [ev-50, ev-51].
  - IBM already sells "living" documentation and equivalence tests [ev-54, ev-55].
  - Kodesage pitches the same wedge [ev-58].
  - Gartner now has an MQ category for this market [ev-68].
- **Accuracy and adoption barriers.**
  - A pure-LLM extractor scored F1 0.73 [ev-39].
  - EPAM rates one of its own use cases' accuracy "Medium" [ev-42].
  - 30% of financial firms ban gen AI, and 80% worry about inaccurate output [ev-43, ev-44].

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:** AWS Transform for mainframe (no service fee), IBM watsonx Code Assistant for Z, Google Mainframe Assessment Tool, Kodesage (on-prem, $6.6M seed, pushing into the US), Hypercubic (YC, $5.3M seed), EvolveWare, mLogica.
- **Indirect:** Mechanical Orchard (rewrites with automated verification), and the core providers that own and maintain most banks' legacy code.
- **Services:** Persistent (SASVA), Praxent (insurance), Profound Logic (IBM i documentation), EPAM, and Trinity Millennium and other specialist BRE consultancies.
- **Manual:** SMEs, overlap hiring, retirees re-hired as contractors, retiree consulting firms.
- **Founder-listed but not verified:** Swimm and TechChannel pages blocked the fetcher (403), so they are not cited. Large systems integrators are covered only through EPAM, Persistent and DXC material.

## Prospects (primary ICP: mid-size US regional banks)

- **Count and sources:** 21 US community and regional banks, all from their own websites.
- **Problem signal:** only Bangor Savings Bank has one (an in-house IBM i/RPG job posting).
- **Size band:** Tri Counties, Peoples, Mechanics, First Mid and Mid Penn may exceed 2,000 employees.
- **Contacts:** 3 named.
  - Bar Harbor: SVP, CIO and Director of Operations.
  - Exchange Bank: Chief Banking Officer.
  - Mechanics Bank: EVP, COO.
- **Not captured:** Washington Trust and Bank of Marin list EVP CIO roles, but not as a quotable name plus title.

Because of the outsourcing finding, qualify each bank for code it owns in-house before any outreach.

  - No first-hand forum complaints from mid-size bank or credit union staff.
