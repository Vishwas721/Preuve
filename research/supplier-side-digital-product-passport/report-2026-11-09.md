# Supplier-side digital product passport credentials for non-EU SME suppliers: research report (2026-11-09)

Idea: `c00f7a65-c1b0-4ad6-87a2-0e9fb9b586f9` · Method v3 · Pack: `pack-2026-11-09.json` · Import: 88 verified, 0 unverifiable, 0 rejected

## Bottom line

The underlying pain is real. EU buyers need digital product passport (DPP), battery-passport and CBAM (carbon border) data that only their suppliers hold. Suppliers already answer repeated, differently formatted requests through PDFs, spreadsheets and email, and they worry about exposing sensitive figures.

The proposed wedge is weaker than the idea assumes:
- "Enter once, share with many" already exists and is free for suppliers (TrusTrace, Retraced, Higg FEM).
- Selective disclosure already exists in Circularise, in the open UN Transparency Protocol (UNTP) and in the EU's own O3CI module for steel.
- Only batteries have a hard deadline (18 Feb 2027), and even that first passport excludes carbon-footprint, recycled-content and due-diligence data.
- Textile passports are expected around 2028–2029.

Most of the evidence is trade press and vendor content. No survey was found of how many hours Indian small suppliers spend on these requests, or how many they receive.

## What supports the problem

- **Readiness is low.** In a Commission survey of 91 mostly large battery companies, only 15% were very confident value-chain data would be available by Feb 2027, and 63% named data availability the biggest challenge [ev-23].
- **EU buyers are already asking:**
  - They expect to request passport figures from suppliers during 2026 [ev-21].
  - They are sending questionnaires before any delegated act exists [ev-11].
  - They write data warranties and audit rights into contracts; slow suppliers risk being replaced [ev-22].
- **Supplier effort is high and repeated:**
  - One textile supplier needs up to 15 days to gather the data for a single order [ev-03].
  - Some factories are audited up to 200 times a year [ev-04].
  - Ten differently formatted buyer requests can turn 2 hours into 2+ days (a 2017 vendor illustration) [ev-01].
  - Suppliers get near-identical spreadsheets from every customer [ev-06], through a mix of platforms and Excel toolkits [ev-02].
- **The cost is mostly staff time:**
  - Working time to gather and update supplier figures, not software [ev-07].
  - Roughly half a role in an exporting small firm [ev-08].
  - Smaller Indian makers carry the same burden without the teams large producers have [ev-09, ev-10].
- **Sensitivity is a real barrier:**
  - Suppliers fear competitors and brand price pressure [ev-33].
  - Competitive concerns have slowed battery data sharing [ev-32].
  - Indian steel small firms worry about disclosing supplier, cost and production data [ev-35].
  - Battery figures that used to travel in a bilateral datasheet become public via the QR code [ev-30].
  - Exporters are told to protect trade secrets in DPP data clauses [ev-31].
  - Data reluctance is described as a trust problem [ev-34].
- **Shared assessments save time.** One Retail Hub reports about 70% time savings and cuts each retailer assessment from 3–4 weeks to 4–6 days [ev-44, ev-45].
- **Adjacent pressure is already biting.** CBAM paperwork has cost some Indian steel exporters their small EU buyers [ev-24].

## What argues against it

- **Timing:**
  - No ESPR delegated act was adopted by mid-2026, and the dates have shifted before [ev-25].
  - Textile, aluminium and tyre acts are expected in Q3–Q4 2027, with at least 18 months before they apply [ev-26].
  - Textile DPPs are expected mid to end 2029 [ev-27].
  - The Feb 2027 battery passport excludes carbon, recycled-content and due-diligence data [ev-28].
- **Supplier-side incumbents exist:**
  - TrusTrace is free for suppliers: one profile shared with all connected clients, 50+ brands and 50,000+ supplier users [ev-42, ev-43].
  - Retraced: "once data is cleanly entered … it does not need to be reprocessed for every new brand" [ev-48].
  - Higg FEM is completed once and accepted by 300+ brands [ev-46, ev-47]; verified results can be shared instead of re-verifying [ev-39].
- **Selective disclosure exists:**
  - Circularise's disclosure layer [ev-52].
  - UNTP's selective redaction of upstream credentials [ev-41].
  - The EU's O3CI module protects sensitive steel emissions data [ev-54].
  - TRACE4EU/EBSI pilots verifiable credentials for battery data [ev-53].
- **Brand-funded onboarding already works.** One German brand onboarded 80 tier-1 and 160 tier-2 suppliers in three months [ev-50]. Sutlej Textiles (India) reached 75-product DPP coverage in one month [ev-51].
- **The hard part may not be repeated entry:**
  - Indian garment exporters hold about 60% of the required data [ev-12].
  - Tier-2 data is a supplier-engagement problem, not a software one [ev-14].
  - The DPP itself is meant to end repeated data provision [ev-59].
- **Schema risk.** No solution can claim end-to-end compliance until the rules are final [ev-29].

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:**
  - TrusTrace: free supplier portal and One Retail Hub.
  - Retraced: supplier network, enter once for every brand.
  - Circularise: disclosure layer.
  - DPP Hero: battery passports from EUR 49 per month [ev-55].
  - AWARE: PDEXCIL deal with Indian powerloom small firms, who keep control of their data; adoption not yet proven [ev-56, ev-57].
- **Indirect:** Higg FEM (Cascale/Worldly), UN Transparency Protocol, EU CBAM O3CI module, TRACE4EU (EBSI).
- **Service:**
  - GreenSutra maps one evidence base to each buyer questionnaire [ev-58].
  - GreenStitch is Sutlej's DPP partner [ev-51].
- **Manual:** spreadsheets, PDFs and email [ev-02, ev-15, ev-16, ev-17].
- **Founder-listed competitors:**
  - TrusTrace and Circularise are evidenced.
  - Renoon appears only as a timeline source [ev-26].
  - No product evidence was gathered for Osapiens or Sourcemap.
