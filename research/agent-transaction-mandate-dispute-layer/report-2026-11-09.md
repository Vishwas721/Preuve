# Research report: Mandate record and dispute-evidence layer for AI-agent transactions

Idea id: `5083be34-10c6-4011-a6e9-ab9085ff066d` · Method v3 · 2026-11-09
Import: 48 verified, 1 unverifiable, 0 rejected (49 evidence items)

## Bottom line

The gap is real: agent disputes are filed under ordinary reason codes, the networks have no mandate field, and mandate records mostly don't reach merchants [ev-02, ev-03, ev-22]. But the evidence against building now is strong. Agent purchase volume is tiny (about 3% of transactions by merchants' own estimate, and ChatGPT Instant Checkout was retired) [ev-17, ev-18]. No public data counts agent disputes at all [ev-19]. Incumbents (Mastercard Verifiable Intent, AP2, PayPal, Stripe, Chargebacks911, Worldpay, Amex) are each building some part of the "neutral record" [ev-23, ev-25, ev-26, ev-12, ev-38, ev-11]. Treat this as a watch-and-interview idea, not a build-now idea. The founder's own "cheapest validation" step (count real agent disputes at 10 PSPs/merchants) is exactly what the public evidence cannot answer.

## What supports the problem

- **No agent signal in dispute data.** Disputed agent purchases use existing codes (Visa 10.4, 13.1, 13.3), and "Nothing in the dispute system labels a transaction as agent-made" [ev-03]. Public Visa/Mastercard guides have no agent reason code, no mandate-content field and no decision-trace representment standard [ev-02].
- **Non-fraud disputes have no answer.** Worldpay: claims that the agent misunderstood or acted outside its authority are dispute claims, not fraud claims, and "Today's frameworks don't yet have a clean answer for who absorbs that" [ev-07]. Without a signed mandate, "issuers tend to side with the cardholder" [ev-08].
- **Merchant pays by default.** The merchant stays merchant of record under ACP and pays unless it wins representment [ev-05, ev-36]. 60% of acquirers put chargeback responsibility on merchants [ev-15]. 93% of merchants say agent providers should bear agent-error losses, and only 28% offer their full range to agents [ev-10].
- **Mandate records don't persist or arrive.** AP2 defines no mandate retention or archive, so evidence may be gone by dispute time [ev-24]. "most consent records still do not reach merchants automatically" [ev-22]. A dispute vendor says current tools can't capture the mandate [ev-04].
- **Industry asks for this evidence.** Checkout.com lists agent IDs, signatures, mandate creation and approval events, and execution traces as the new evidence set [ev-35]. Chargebacks911 recommends recording authorisation at delegation time [ev-13]. 45% of acquirers (56% in the US) say liability frameworks need redesign [ev-14].

## What argues against it

- **Volume is tiny.** Merchants say only 3% of transactions involve AI agents [ev-17]. "almost none" of shoppers plan to buy through agents [ev-37]. Visa pilots were in the hundreds of transactions in late 2025 [ev-20]. 77% of acquirers are waiting for signals [ev-16].
- **The flagship agent checkout retreated.** ChatGPT Instant Checkout was retired in March 2026, and fewer than about 30 Shopify merchants ever turned it on [ev-18]. In most flows the user still confirms the final purchase [ev-21], which limits the "my agent bought it without me" dispute.
- **Fraud claims are already covered.** On properly authenticated, tokenized agent payments the issuer carries fraud risk [ev-06].
- **Incumbents are building the record.**
  - Mastercard Verifiable Intent is a protocol-agnostic, tamper-resistant record "that all parties can consult if a dispute arises" [ev-23].
  - Google AP2 [ev-42] and PayPal [ev-25] position mandates as dispute-grade evidence.
  - Stripe scopes agent tokens to reduce disputes [ev-26].
  - Chargebacks911 already markets agent consent trails [ev-12], and Worldpay is building agent detection plus dispute evidence [ev-38].
  - Amex simply covers erroneous registered-agent purchases [ev-11].
  - Worldpay argues "only the card networks can close the liability gap at scale" [ev-09].
- **Mandates don't settle intent.** A signed cart proves what was bought, not that it matched what the user meant [ev-34], so a mandate record may not win "wrong item within mandate" disputes.

## Unknowns

| Unknown | Status | What we found |
|---|---|---|
| How merchants distinguish agent disputes | answered | They can't today: no label and no agent reason code [ev-03, ev-02]. Acquirers are starting to detect agent traffic upstream [ev-38]. |
| Revenue/labour lost per month to agent disputes | partially answered | The merchant bears the loss by default [ev-05]. General chargeback labour costs are visible ($40/case freelancer, 199 hours saved) [ev-27, ev-44]. **No evidence found** for agent-specific losses [ev-19]. |
| Who owns the budget | partially answered | At the mid-market merchants found, CX/support managers or freelancers handled chargebacks, not a Head of Fraud [ev-28, ev-29]. The existing paid line is contingency-priced automation (25% of recoveries) [ev-30]. |
| Data elements networks/issuers require | partially answered | No agent-specific requirement has been published [ev-02]. Practitioners name mandate, agent ID, signatures and execution traces [ev-35, ev-08]. No primary rulebook text was fetchable. |
| Chargeback platforms in use | answered | Alert networks plus automation vendors (Ethoca/Verifi, Chargeflow) [ev-43, ev-40], plus manual CX handling [ev-28]. |
| How often agent purchases are disputed | partially answered | **No public data** [ev-19]. Volume of agent purchases is small [ev-17, ev-18]. |

The import also added two new unknowns: whether networks will deliver mandate records directly (leaving no room for a neutral layer), and what share of agent disputes are "wrong item within mandate" cases.

## Method notes

- **Queries:** 20, including 4 contradiction searches (adoption volume, network liability protection, "no increase so far", Instant Checkout uptake).
- **Source mix:** dispute and PSP vendor blogs (Chargeflow, chargeback.io, Checkout.com, Worldpay, ChargebackHelp), news (The Paypers, Forkast, PYMNTS), industry newsletters and analysis (Tinyfish, Stellagent, RSM, Alhena), one practitioner Substack, vendor case studies, one job posting, and the AP2 and Stripe sites.
- **Bias:** many sources sell dispute tooling and have an interest in describing a "gap".
- **Survey figures:** the PYMNTS, Ravelin and Darwinium numbers are quoted as reported by secondary pages, not taken from the primary reports.
- **Excluded source:** a Tinyfish "dispute analyst" interview is a composite character, so it was not used.
- **Unfetchable:** Justt, Midigator, Skyfire, Mastercard's chargeback-cost page, Visa press coverage and several merchant about pages.
- **Unverifiable item:** 1 item imported as unverifiable.
