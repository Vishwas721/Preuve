- Idea: `04f65536-87f5-4fc4-8c87-8d993674af12` (http://localhost:3100/ideas/04f65536-87f5-4fc4-8c87-8d993674af12)

- Import: 64 verified, 0 unverifiable, 0 rejected.

## Bottom line

The commission pain is real but narrower than the idea assumes. Faire takes about 17-18% all-in on every marketplace reorder [ev-02], and makers complain about it [ev-08].

Four things shrink the opportunity:

1. Retailers a brand brings in itself already order at 0% commission through Faire Direct [ev-03].
2. Faire's terms forbid moving retailers that Faire sourced off the platform [ev-14].
3. Since April 2026, native Shopify B2B with Net 30/60 terms is on all paid plans, not only Plus [ev-16][ev-18].
4. Several Shopify apps launched in 2026 already send pre-filled reorder drafts [ev-20][ev-22].

The evidence supports a real pain but not a clear gap. Differentiation and willingness to pay $59/month are unproven.

## What supports the problem

- **Fees are high and recurring.** Faire charges 15% on every marketplace order, a $10 new-retailer fee and 1.9-3.5% processing [ev-01]. Reorders come to about 17-18% all-in, for the whole life of each stockist relationship [ev-02][ev-09].
- **Reorders cost more than direct wholesale.** A DTC finance analysis finds the 15% reorder commission is higher than what a sales rep takes, even for a book made up entirely of reorders [ev-04].
- **Makers feel the pain.**
  - One calls the 25% take high on prices that are already halved, but feels dependent on Faire [ev-08].
  - Another says their own products are buried under mass-produced items in Faire search [ev-13][ev-32].
- **Brands already steer reorders away from Faire.**
  - Ladyfingers Letterpress gives 15% off orders placed on its own wholesale site [ev-38].
  - Idlewild Co. offers direct-account discounts and Net 30 [ev-40][ev-41].
  - Guttersnipe Press and Sweet Water Decor run their own wholesale logins [ev-57][ev-48].
  - Guides call "Faire for discovery, direct for best accounts" common practice [ev-27].
- **The work costs labour.**
  - A brand hires a coordinator to enter orders across Shopify B2B, Faire and FashionGo, and to handle reordering [ev-33][ev-34].
  - Another brand takes reorders by email [ev-55].
- **Brands lack retailer contact details.** Brands don't get contact details for retailers Faire sourced [ev-31].

## What argues against it

- **Faire Direct is already 0%.** Retailers a brand brings in itself carry no commission [ev-03].
  - The recommended strategy is to move high-value accounts to Faire Direct, not to a separate portal [ev-06].
  - Some brands send all wholesale through a Faire Direct link [ev-64].
- **Anti-circumvention rules.** Brands can't move retailers Faire sourced off the platform to save commission [ev-14][ev-11]. Those are exactly the accounts that still pay 15%.
- **Shopify B2B is no longer Plus-only.**
  - Basic, Grow and Advanced plans have had company profiles, payment terms and volume pricing since April 2026 [ev-16].
  - That includes Net 30/60 at checkout [ev-18].
  - Commentators say this made many B2B apps redundant [ev-17].
- **Reorder automation already exists.**
  - Repeatly and Reorder Nudge send pre-filled reorder drafts with invoices [ev-20][ev-22].
  - B2BOptix suggests reorders inside the store [ev-23].
  - Faire's own CRM automates reorder reminders [ev-15].
- **Retailers prefer Faire.** Even shops a brand found itself may prefer Faire's convenience [ev-10]. Retailers praise its breadth and terms [ev-35][ev-36].
- **Reorders are infrequent.** Boutiques reorder once or twice a year, a 20-35% year-one reorder rate [ev-05]. That limits the savings.
- **Some makers accept the fee.**
  - Some treat the commission as a substitute for advertising [ev-12].
  - For brands with fewer than 50 accounts, Faire is judged worth its fee [ev-07].

## Unknowns

- They use Faire or Faire Direct, email, or their own Shopify wholesale site [ev-03][ev-55][ev-38][ev-57].
- The money cost on Faire is about 17-18% per reorder [ev-02].
- Brands pay coordinators for this work [ev-33]. No hours figure was found.

**Reorder frequency and revenue share: partially answered.**
- One analytical estimate puts reorders at once or twice a year [ev-05].
- No evidence found on what share of revenue reorders make up.

**Why brands keep reorders on Faire, and the barriers to moving them: answered.**
- Retailer convenience and terms [ev-10][ev-35][ev-36][ev-37].
- Faire's anti-circumvention rules [ev-14][ev-11][ev-31].
- Makers feel dependent on Faire [ev-08].
- For retailers a brand brings in itself, Faire Direct already solves the cost [ev-03].

**Who the buyer is and what budget exists: partially answered.**

- No budget evidence found. Stockist's $150/month is the only reference point [ev-25].

**Value of pre-filled orders, case-pack rules and Net terms to stockists: partially answered.**
- Retailers value terms [ev-36][ev-37].
- Reorder-draft apps exist but have no reviews [ev-20][ev-21].
- No evidence found on case-pack rules.

**Strengths and weaknesses of Shopify wholesale apps: partially answered, from vendor pages only.**
- Native B2B makes many apps redundant [ev-17].
- Gaps remain in rep ordering and quoting [ev-19].

**How brands handle Net 30/60 terms: partially answered.**
- Some brands offer their own Net 30 [ev-41]. Others require prepayment [ev-53].
- Shopify now supports net terms natively [ev-18], and Resolve exists [ev-28].
- Faire's Net 60 draws retailers [ev-37][ev-48].

**New unknowns added:**
- What share of reorder revenue comes from retailers Faire sourced (locked to Faire) versus retailers the brand sourced (already 0%)?
- What would let a portal beat free native B2B and the 2026 reorder apps?

## Method notes

- **Sources:** 24 web searches. Pages were read with Preuve's fetcher, and quotes were checked mechanically against the saved text.
- **Blocked sources:** Faire.com (support, blog, brand pages), Home Accents Today, digitalcommerce360 and cahoot.ai returned 403 or were blocked. None of them is cited, so Faire's fees and policies come through third-party guides.
- **Source mix:** industry guides and vendor blogs, the Shopify changelog and App Store listings, one job posting, a retailer app-review page, and brand wholesale and about pages.
- **Not found:** Reddit threads, a retailer survey, and any pricing signal for a reorder portal.
