# Research report: Source-Level Web Accessibility Remediation for E-commerce (Overlay Alternative)

Idea: `1ae4de29-46a4-450a-89c9-671ab9d78db9` · Date: 2026-11-10 · Method v3 · Import: 100 verified / 0 unverifiable / 0 rejected

## Bottom line

The problem is real and well evidenced. US website accessibility lawsuits are frequent, rising and concentrated on e-commerce, with many repeat defendants. The overlay widgets that many target stores pay for do not prevent suits [ev-07][ev-01][ev-02][ev-28]. The proposed solution, however, is already a crowded category: at least six Shopify apps fix theme source code today, at $49–$599/month, the same price range as this idea [ev-38][ev-42][ev-43][ev-46][ev-40]. Notice-and-cure laws, weak early EU enforcement and Shopify's own built-in accessibility features reduce the urgency to buy [ev-20][ev-63][ev-18][ev-49]. The open question is no longer whether the problem exists, but why a merchant would pick this product over the existing source-fix apps.

## What supports the problem

- **Volume and growth.** Seyfarth counts 3,117 federal website accessibility suits in 2025, up 27% [ev-07]. UsableNet counts 5,000+ digital suits, nearly 70% against e-commerce [ev-01]. A UsableNet-cited analysis says 73% of suits target businesses under $25M revenue [ev-41].
- **Repeat suits.** 1,427 of the 2025 suits hit previously sued companies; 46% of federal cases involved repeat defendants [ev-02]. A Shopify merchant reports three suits in four years and tens of thousands paid in settlements [ev-09].
- **Overlays don't protect.** About 38% of 2025 defendants already had a tool installed [ev-28]. Widget users were sued every month, peaking above 150 a month [ev-03][ev-06]. A merchant installed accessiBe after a suit and was sued again [ev-32]. The FTC order bars accessiBe from claiming its overlay makes sites WCAG-compliant [ev-22]. The European Disability Forum and the international accessibility professionals' association (IAAP) state that overlays do not meet EU law [ev-27]. Practitioners have signed letters against overlays (400+ in 2021 [ev-23], 1,000+ later per a vendor [ev-26]).
- **Small stores can't fix it themselves.** Shopify says small sellers lack money for specialised developers [ev-16]. A very small sued store doesn't know whom to hire [ev-14]. In an AudioEye survey, 64% of organisations that handle accessibility in-house lack the expertise [ev-29]. A three-employee brand faced a $30,000 demand [ev-17].
- **Cost of today's fix.** Merchant quotes run about $4k–$25k [ev-11]. An agency estimates $3k–$10k for an audit plus $5k–$25k for fixes [ev-55]. Settlements typically run $15k–$75k [ev-30].
- **EU pressure.** The European Accessibility Act covers online shops [ev-62][ev-66]. The Dutch regulator has written to e-commerce firms worldwide [ev-65]. A Swedish apparel shop declares itself in scope [ev-78].
- **Apparel and beauty are targets.** An apparel retailer's complaint became a $5.15M class settlement [ev-31]. Product-image alt text is the top failure in small-store audits, including clothing brands [ev-53][ev-54].

## What argues against it

- **Crowded direct competition.** TestParty (from $99/month for scanning; $599/month managed) [ev-38][ev-40][ev-47], Curbcut [ev-42], AccessComply [ev-43], Remedy [ev-44], AccessFix [ev-45] and Patrol (from $200/month) [ev-46] already fix code or give code fixes. Several of these apps launched in 2026. An AI store assistant (Fudge) also drafts fixes in theme code [ev-48].
- **Lower urgency.**
  - Missouri's 2026 law gives businesses that fix issues within 90 days of notice a presumption that later claims are abusive [ev-20].
  - Shopify is lobbying for notice-and-cure reform [ev-19].
  - Some states are adding small-business safe harbors [ev-05].
  - California courts largely exclude online-only stores [ev-08].
- **Weak early EU enforcement.** 45% of organisations report nothing changed after the Act took effect [ev-63]. Denmark issued a warning with no fines [ev-64]. EU micro-businesses that provide services are exempt [ev-61].
- **The platform covers part of it.** Shopify provides accessible themes and AI alt-text generation [ev-18], requires a Lighthouse accessibility score of 90+ for Theme Store themes [ev-51], and its standard checkout is platform-controlled and WCAG-tested [ev-49][ev-50]. That weakens the "fix the checkout" part of the wedge on Shopify.
- **The switching trigger is weaker than assumed.** Some overlay buyers praise accessiBe's price and setup [ev-34]. A merchant sees the overlay debate as split 50/50 [ev-13]. Sued merchants distrust monthly-fee compliance vendors (inference) [ev-67].
- **Effort may be modest for mid-market brands,** per one agency [ev-56]. Automated scans catch only about 25% of WCAG criteria, which limits a mostly automated product [ev-58].
- **Bias caveat.** Most lawsuit statistics come from vendors (UsableNet, AudioEye, TestParty) that sell accessibility products.

## Method notes

- **Queries:** 30 WebSearch queries (listed in the pack's `meta.queries`), including 7 contradiction searches: lawsuit decline, Shopify theme requirements, AI self-fix, AudioEye guarantees, EU Act enforcement, Shopify Sidekick and Horizon, and small-business safe harbors.
- **Quotes:** every quote was copied from `research fetch` output and checked locally before import. All 100 verified.
- **Source mix:**
  - Industry and trade publications plus law-firm analyses
  - Vendor sites and Shopify App Store listings
  - Shopify Community forum threads
  - App reviews (AppNavigator)
  - An AudioEye-produced report distributed by Stacker
  - The EU disability groups' (EDF/IAAP) statement
  - Company accessibility statements and home pages
- **Could not verify:** FTC press release (blocked by robots.txt); Level Access SODAR survey and Deque EU Act blog (403 / robots.txt); AudioEye pages (HTTP 429); job postings at Tarte, Knix and MERIT (expired, 404 or 429). The idea's "1,000+ signatories" claim rests on a vendor citation [ev-26]; the fetched 2021 article says 400+ [ev-23].
- **Privacy:** no personal names or usernames appear in any evidence. Brands named after people were excluded from prospects. Quotes were chosen to avoid names, including the pseudonyms in Shopify's article.
- **Limits:** lawsuit statistics are largely from vendors with commercial interests. There is no data on buyer budgets or internal developer hours.
