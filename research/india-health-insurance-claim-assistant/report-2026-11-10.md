## Bottom line
The problem is real, large and on regulators' record. Health claim complaints run to about 1.18 lakh a year on Bima Bharosa, and Ombudsman complaints about rejected or short-paid claims are rising (35,186 in FY26). Partial settlements come from exactly the clause types this tool would explain: sub-limits, co-pay and room rent. Expert help measurably improves outcomes. Willingness to pay is the weak point. The Ombudsman is free and claimants do win alone, distributors give claim help to their own customers for free, and general chatbots already draft complaints. The leading paid service takes only 3–4 of every 100 cases. Treat the evidence as strong for the problem and weak for the pricing.

## What supports the problem
- **Volume:** Health insurers received 1,17,979 Bima Bharosa complaints in FY26 [ev-01]. About 69% of general/health grievances are claim-related, and health makes up about 64% of Ombudsman complaints [ev-03]. Rejection complaints at the Ombudsman rose to 35,186 in FY26 [ev-07]. 95% of health complaints to the Ombudsman in 2023-24 concerned claim rejections [ev-14]. Insurance Samadhan says 88% of the health complaints it handles are about claim rejection [ev-12].
- **Deductions are clause-driven and opaque:** IRDAI says claims are partly settled because of sub-limits, co-pay, deductibles, room-rent limits and non-medical expenses [ev-02]. Survey respondents report deductions with no clear explanation [ev-10] and surprise exclusions [ev-11]. The secretary general of the Insurance Brokers Association of India cites "unwarranted deductions from hospital bills" [ev-22]. Room-rent proportionate deductions recalculate the whole bill [ev-20]. An unofficial explainer says cuts beyond room rent breach the 2024 master circular yet still happen (unconfirmed) [ev-21]. IRDAI's chairman says fully settled amounts are "lower than expected" [ev-34].
- **Self-reported frequency:** In LocalCircles surveys, 33% of claimants said their claim was partially approved and 36% said it was rejected, in both cases for reasons they considered invalid [ev-08]. A later survey put it at about 4 in 10 [ev-10].
- **A painful, long escalation:** Documented cases run 10 to 22 months and involve repeated document requests, template-style denials and grievance officers who don't respond [ev-15, ev-17, ev-19, ev-23, ev-11]. Policyholders struggle to use the grievance mechanisms without help [ev-24]. Badly drafted complaints lose winnable cases [ev-39].
- **Help works:** Assisted resubmissions reverse 32–67% of rejections, against 6% for people doing it alone [ev-29]. About 71% of FY2024-25 Ombudsman complaints were resolved in the policyholder's favour [ev-05].
- **A paid market exists:** Insurance Samadhan charged ₹500 plus 9% of the recovered amount (2020) [ev-25] and reports ₹160 crore recovered across 18,000+ cases [ev-27].
- **Intermediaries pay for this work:** A broker hires claims staff to review insurer denials "as per policy terms" and contest them [ev-42]. A group-health intermediary pays staff to upload claim files and chase missing documents [ev-41]. Rejected claims pull HR into hours of escalations [ev-44, ev-46].

## What argues against it
- **Rejection is a minority and falling:** Repudiations fell to about 8% of claims in FY25, from about 11% [ev-31]. Every rejection now needs a committee's approval [ev-32]. Top insurers settle 97–99% of claims by count [ev-35]. Policybazaar reports 94% of claims approved [ev-28].
- **Many rejections are valid:** A large share is the policyholder's fault [ev-47]. Denials over misunderstood exclusions are "contractually aligned with policy wording" [ev-45].
- **Free alternatives cap willingness to pay:** The Ombudsman is free [ev-36], and a claimant won alone calling it "a straightforward process" [ev-18]. Free chatbots used with published prompts already draft complaints [ev-38].
- **Narrow success-fee economics:** The leading service selects 3–4 of every 100 cases [ev-26]. The Ombudsman win rate fell to about 54% in FY26 [ev-06]. The average paid claim is only about ₹28,910 [ev-33].
- **Regulatory risk:** IRDAI tells the public to deal only with insurers or registered intermediaries, in a notice about fee-seeking callers [ev-40]. I found no primary notice specifically about claim consultants.
- **Some of the value is already mandated:** Insurers must already state the reason and the clause for a rejection [ev-48].

## How it's solved today
- **Direct:** Policygaido (GaidoAI) lets you upload a policy and flags exclusions and proportionate-deduction triggers, before a claim is made [ev-54].
- **Indirect:** Distributors' claim desks: Policybazaar's claim support and Claim Samadhan Diwas [ev-53], Ditto [ev-51], PolicyX [ev-68], nyvo's claim-support guarantee [ev-57], OneAssure's PolicyPal chatbot [ev-55, ev-56]. One user said Policybazaar's help ended after one reconsideration [ev-16]. Free general chatbots draft complaints [ev-38, ev-37].
- **Service:** Insurance Samadhan on a success fee [ev-25, ev-27, ev-26]. Employee-benefits brokers' claims teams [ev-42, ev-41, ev-74].
- **Manual:** Claimants escalate on their own to the insurer's grievance officer, Bima Bharosa and the free Ombudsman, checking the cited clause against their policy by hand [ev-36, ev-18, ev-49].
- **Founder-listed:** Insurance Samadhan and Policybazaar are covered above. Hospital TPA desks and lawyers have no evidence of their own here.

**Prospects:** 19 intermediaries for the agents/small-brokers target group, with no contacts recorded. Small ones: nyvo, Okbima, Algates Insurance, Bimacafe, Probus, Intact, RiskBirbal. Distributors: PolicyX, Ditto, InsuranceDekho, Coverfox, OneAssure. Group-benefit brokers and channels: Prudent, Loop Health, Plum, Onsurity, Nova Benefits, Pazcare, Turtlemint. Several sit outside the 1–10 staff size band and are listed as channel partners. Many already offer claim help, so they could be buyers or competitors.
