# Report: Meal-plan generation engine for dietitians (with Indian/regional cuisine)

Idea id: `b22edc0c-63ca-4989-814b-b89d3d420056` · Method v3 · 2026-11-07 · Pack: `pack-2026-11-07.json`
Import: 97 verified, 0 unverifiable, 0 rejected (after one quote fix on re-import).

## Bottom line

The pain is real but the gap is mostly closed. Dietitians do spend hours on hand-built plans, and Western tools serve South Asian food poorly. But That Clean Life already generates plans from calories and macros and plugs into Practice Better. In India, at least four vendors (Nutrena, mealPlan.fit, WellnessZ, Hint Expert) already sell AI or template plan generation on Indian and regional food databases for Rs 499-2,999/month. Nutrena already offers almost exactly this wedge (a 7-day plan in seconds, 8,000+ Indian dishes across 28 state cuisines, white-label PDFs) at Rs 999 or $25/month. The idea's claims of a "clearly documented feature gap" and "weak Indian coverage everywhere" are not supported. The opening that remains is narrower: diaspora dietitians abroad who use Western tools with poor South Asian recipe coverage. Even there, several leading South Asian-focused RDs deliberately avoid standalone meal plans.

## What supports the problem

- **Manual plans take a long time.** Estimates range from 30-60 minutes per plan, a vendor claim [ev-02], to 2-3 hours [ev-03] and 4-6 hours in one coach's own practice [ev-01]. One dietitian called plan-building time her "biggest pain point" [ev-11], and another spent hours per plan even with software [ev-05]. Most of these figures come from vendors or people with an affiliate interest, and no independent survey was found.
- **The manual process** is recipe research plus typing into Word [ev-12]. In India it is Word diet charts, spreadsheets and WhatsApp [ev-13]. Allergens are checked by hand [ev-02, ev-03].
- **Western dietitians struggle with South Asian food.** A BC Dietitians webinar says dietitians feel unprepared, and quotes a client asking for "my meal plan with regular Indian foods" [ev-16]. An Indian-American RDN describes a "gap in understanding of our food" among other dietitians [ev-17]. A BDA article says South Asian patients struggle when advice asks them to abandon their foods [ev-18], and that "South Asian" is not one diet [ev-19]. An Australian study finds staple food preferences resist change [ev-20].
- **Databases have gaps.** USDA-based databases are poor for home-cooked Indian food [ev-21], and research notes gaps in Indian food composition data [ev-22, ev-23, ev-25].
- **General LLMs are not trusted for this.** LLM plans scored far below a dietitian's, including for an Indian vegetarian profile [ev-26]. Researchers advise expert-facing use only [ev-27]. AI plans undercounted energy by about 700 kcal [ev-28], and a builder reports GPT-4 invents calorie counts [ev-29]. This supports a curated-database approach, but competitors already take it.
- **Practice software is weak at meal planning.** Practice Better's and Healthie's meal planning are described as weak by a competitor [ev-34, ev-35]. Practices pay for an add-on on top [ev-49].

## What argues against it

- **The "feature gap" is already filled abroad.** That Clean Life generates meal plans "in seconds based on calories, macros, and diet type" [ev-30, ev-33] and integrates natively with Practice Better [ev-36]. Nutrium users report less time spent on planning [ev-37].
- **The India wedge already exists.**
  - Nutrena: 7-day plan in seconds [ev-38], 8,000+ Indian dishes across 28 state cuisines [ev-39], Rs 999 or $25/month with white-label PDFs [ev-40].
  - mealPlan.fit: AI drafts in under 2 minutes [ev-43] and an IFCT database [ev-42].
  - WellnessZ: AI plans from goals, preferences and calories [ev-46], from Rs 499 [ev-47].
  - Hint Expert: thousands of Indian recipes [ev-45], from Rs 499 [ev-44].
- **Prices.** The idea's floor of Rs 999 / $29 is at or above incumbents [ev-40, ev-44, ev-47]. mealPlan.fit does charge Rs 1,999-2,999 [ev-41], so some willingness to pay exists in India.
- **Some dietitians don't sell plans at all.** Many reject prescriptive meal plans [ev-06, ev-07]. The best-known US Indian-American RDN turns away people who only want a "diet plan" [ev-09]. A Canadian practice with South Asian-cuisine dietitians positions itself against one-size-fits-all meal plans [ev-08]. For part of the primary ICP, the plan is not the main paid deliverable.

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:** That Clean Life ($30/$60 per month) [ev-30, ev-31]; Nutrena [ev-38-40]; mealPlan.fit [ev-41-43]; Hint Expert [ev-44, ev-45]; WellnessZ [ev-46, ev-47]; Promealplan ($49/mo, on its own page) [ev-49]; Kore App [ev-13].
- **Indirect:** Practice Better and Healthie, whose meal planning is weak [ev-34, ev-35, ev-36]; Nutrium [ev-37]; Zoconut [ev-48].
- **Manual:** Word, Excel and WhatsApp [ev-12, ev-13, ev-04].
- **Service:** no dedicated meal-plan-writing services were verified. Upwork and Fiverr listings were unfetchable.
- **Founder-listed tools:** That Clean Life, Nutrium, Practice Better and Promealplan all have evidence above. Excel is covered under manual.

## Prospects (primary ICP: South Asian diaspora dietitians abroad)

18 practices were found, short of the 20+ target. Contacts with name and title come only from the practices' own pages (8 contacts).

- **Australia:** Diet Yumm, Eat Smartly, Ease Dietetics, Motion Health and Rehab Performance.
- **Canada:** Health Stand Nutrition, Healthy Diet, The Dietitian Studio, Nutrition by Avi.
- **US:** Vandana Sheth RDN, Nourishing Expert LLC, Kavitha Felix-Johnson RDN, Disease Prevention LLC. Disease Prevention LLC uses Healthie, which is a technology-fit signal.
- **UK:** Kidney Nutrition Matters, Tabby Dietitian, Sensus Nutrition, Nutrivibes with Alka, DietConsultancy, The Family Wellbeing Dietitian.

Several of these are evidenced only through association or public directories (BDA, Dietitians Australia, Medimap, Dietitian Central, PCRM). A Toronto RD was dropped because its only fetchable page shared a directory domain with another prospect. Most UK and Australian prospects are NHS or clinic dietitians with freelance practices, so their fit as buyers is moderate.
