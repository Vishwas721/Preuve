# Courier weight-dispute and COD audit for Indian D2C sellers: research report (2026-11-05)

Idea: `ec6e4f63-83c6-4808-842f-ec5833872460` · Method v3 · Pack: `pack-2026-11-05.json`
Import result: 93 verified, 1 unverifiable, 0 rejected.

## Bottom line

The problem is real. Weight charges arrive weeks after dispatch, dispute windows are short and auto-accept, and sellers say disputes get rejected even when they have photo proof. The evidence for the business is weaker. Every rupee figure comes from vendors selling a fix. One vendor's worked example puts permanent weight leakage at about 1.4% of courier spend. Aggregators (Shiprocket, Velocity, iCarry, Eshopbox) already auto-dispute or freeze weights themselves. Treat this as "pain confirmed, size of the recoverable pool unproven". The founder's plan to audit 10-15 sellers' exports by hand is exactly the missing test.

## What supports the problem

- **Auto-accept windows.** Shiprocket gives 7 working days and then auto-accepts and debits the wallet [ev-06]. Velocity gives 10 days [ev-17], and dispute windows typically close in 3-7 days [ev-23]. Shiprocket's average weight billing timeline is 21 days [ev-09].
- **Seller pain in their own words.** A small apparel seller uploaded tape and weight images for every parcel and was still charged; their disputes went nowhere [ev-01, ev-02]. Forum users say discrepancies were auto-accepted before they noticed [ev-03, ev-04]. An app reviewer reports 90% of orders flagged, Rs100-150 per extra kg, and needing 2-3 people at 100 orders/day to keep up [ev-05].
- **Vendor loss estimates.** Shipway estimates Rs18-35 per flagged order, or Rs90k-1.75 lakh/month at 5,000 orders, most of it never disputed [ev-22]. Eshopbox estimates Rs10-50 per flagged shipment [ev-21]. Velocity says brands "often end up accepting incorrect charges" [ev-18]. These are all inferences from sellers of competing solutions.
- **Share of shipments flagged.** Estimates run 4-9% of AWBs [ev-24] to 5-20% [ev-27].
- **Fake RTOs.** Sellers and brands report fake "customer refused" remarks and fake delivery attempts on COD orders [ev-39, ev-40, ev-41]. The Hatke bore RTO charges on refused COD orders [ev-53].
- **COD reconciliation is manual and staffed.** Most merchants reconcile "by gut feel" [ev-32]. One estimate is 3-5 working days a month of finance time [ev-30]. Companies hire accounts staff for it [ev-37], and remittance gaps are "a recoverable loss that manual reconciliation consistently misses" [ev-36].

## What argues against it

- **Recoverable money may be small.** Terra's worked example has 40% of flagged AWBs won with evidence and permanent leakage of Rs84,000 on Rs58 lakh/month of spend [ev-25]. COD exceptions run 0.4-1.2% of COD value [ev-26]. Prozo's 2-5% recovery claim applies to brands shipping 10,000+ parcels, which is above this ICP [ev-28].
- **Many charges are valid.** Shiprocket says most discrepancies come from sellers declaring dead weight instead of volumetric weight [ev-07].
- **Incumbents are already moving in:**
  - Shiprocket offers Weight Freeze, AWB image checks and a "zero weight discrepancy" assurance program [ev-11, ev-12, ev-13].
  - Velocity auto-disputes and handles 40-50% of claims without the brand [ev-17, ev-19].
  - Eshopbox and iCarry auto-raise disputes [ev-20, ev-43].
  - JetPost debits within 48h of dispatch [ev-29].
- **The WhatsApp RTO wedge is already covered.** Shiprocket already messages buyers on WhatsApp and has a "Fake attempt" escalation [ev-42].
- **Sellers are alerted.** Shiprocket's SOP describes email and SMS reminders throughout the window [ev-08]. That weakens the "nobody noticed" story, though sellers dispute it [ev-04].
- **Manual COD reconciliation can be cheap.** A Google Sheet and 30 minutes a week catches most COD discrepancies at small volume [ev-33].
- **Per-parcel photos may be impractical.** One aggregator advises photographing only risky parcels [ev-45].
- **COD fraud may be rare.** It is described as "rare but damaging" [ev-31].

## How it's solved today

- **Indirect:**
  - Shiprocket's dispute flow, Weight Freeze and assurance program.
  - Velocity's auto-dispute.
  - Eshopbox's overcharge protection.
  - iCarry's auto-trigger.
  - JetPost TrueWeight Shield.
  - Shipmozo's D+1 COD dashboard.
  - Delhivery One's claim flow [ev-16].
- **Direct:**
  - ProShip by Prozo (weight workflows).
  - BUSY Recom and Cointab (courier and COD reconciliation).
  - Packing-video tools: HyprPack, VuDrop, ClaimVMS, Unicommerce UniCapture and Flipkart VMS [ev-47 to ev-51].
- **Manual:** Google Sheets matching of Shopify orders to courier remittance files, and manual photos and videos at packing.
- **Service:** in-house accounts executives.

The founder listed Shiprocket's flow, packing videos and spreadsheets; all three have evidence. The claim that "no independent tool works on the seller's side" is only partly true: BUSY Recom, Cointab and the packing-video tools are seller-side, though none combines all four wedges.
