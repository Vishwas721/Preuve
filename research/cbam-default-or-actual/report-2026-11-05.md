# "Default or actual?" CBAM decision pack for small exporters (India to EU): research report

Idea: `3b504d74-ae74-40d9-b914-ca54653fe612` · Method v3 · 2026-11-05 · Pack: `pack-2026-11-05.json`
Import result: **54 verified, 0 unverifiable, 0 rejected.**

## Bottom line

The pain is real and current. Indian MSMEs are getting CBAM data requests, few of them measure emissions,
they depend on suppliers and utilities that don't send data, and some are losing small EU buyers and orders
[ev-01, ev-03, ev-04, ev-09, ev-12]. But the proposed wedge is weak on three counts:

- **The decision step already exists for free.** GreenSutra publishes a free default-vs-verified cost
  calculator for India-origin goods [ev-25], and the EU fastener distributors' association (EFDA) gives
  suppliers a free template with a calculator [ev-22].
- **"Actual" is often not available or not worth much yet.** No accredited CBAM verifier had been listed as
  of October 2026 [ev-29]. For coal-based Indian steel inputs, defaults are close to real emissions [ev-34].
- **The planned channels and payers are taken or unwilling.** SEPC, an export promotion council, already
  partners with CleanCarbon.ai [ev-27]. None of the MSMEs surveyed got help from their buyers [ev-03]. The
  government may reimburse 90% of verification costs, which favours consultants and verifiers [ev-28].

## What supports the problem

- **Buyer pressure and lost business.** Danish and German customers made CBAM reports mandatory for a
  fastener MSME, which then hired a consultant (about Rs 2 lakh a year), got compliance warnings and lost
  orders [ev-01, ev-02]. Smaller EU buyers of 20–25 tonnes have disappeared for one exporter [ev-12]. EU
  orders that normally start in October are being held back [ev-16]. Consignments were held at EU ports and a
  7,000-tonne order was lost, though that claim comes from a CBAM vendor [ev-07].
- **Data gaps.** Only 24% of surveyed MSMEs measure emissions [ev-04]. Data must come from many small vendors
  and utilities [ev-05, ev-09, ev-15]. Only 12% of fastener suppliers returned the EFDA template with specific
  values [ev-21].
- **Defaults are costly and rising.** The mark-up goes from 10% in 2026 to 30% from 2028 [ev-24]. On defaults,
  Indian hot-rolled steel costs about EUR 278 per tonne in Q3 2026 [ev-26], an effective tariff of over 15% on
  hot-rolled coil [ev-35]. GTRI says defaults are often 30–80% above actual emissions [ev-10]. Price cuts of
  15–22% are expected [ev-11].
- **Buyers are starting to select suppliers on carbon data.** Fabory plans to shape its supply base around
  suppliers able to invest [ev-19]. KPMG advises importers to weigh verified data against the default
  liability [ev-30].
- **Consultants are scarce.** Many are still learning and hard to reach [ev-14]. India has few EU-familiar
  auditors [ev-31].

## What argues against it

- **Free calculators exist.** GreenSutra's default-vs-verified calculator [ev-25] does what the idea's
  "cheapest test" proposes, and the EFDA calculator does too [ev-22]. Bossard collects supplier data through
  its own portal [ev-20].
- **Verified data may not pay off.** Indian coal-based DRI defaults are "closer to the physical emissions of
  the process", so verification only removes the mark-up [ev-34]. Verifiers weren't accredited yet as of
  October 2026 [ev-29].
- **Importers don't have to ask.** Declarations on defaults are complete without actual data [ev-32].
  Importers under 50 tonnes a year are exempt, by design for SMEs [ev-33, ev-45].
- **Buyers don't help.** In the IamSMEofIndia survey, none of the firms had received help from buyers [ev-03].
  That argues against the "EU buyers sponsor packs" revenue idea, and against the ICP Preuve picked
  (importers sponsoring suppliers).
- **The channel and subsidy favour incumbents.** SEPC's Carbon Cell runs with CleanCarbon.ai, which claims 200
  exporter clients [ev-27, ev-41]. The proposed 90% reimbursement covers certification and verification
  costs [ev-28].
- **Regulatory risk.** EFDA lobbies to remove fasteners from CBAM [ev-23]. The downstream extension is still a
  proposal [ev-37].

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:** GreenSutra [ev-25, ev-26, ev-29], CleanCarbon.ai [ev-27, ev-40, ev-41] and The
  Sustainability Cloud [ev-42].
- **Indirect:** CBAMBOO on the importer side [ev-38, ev-39]; government support through SEPC's Carbon Cell,
  awareness sessions and the proposed 90% reimbursement [ev-27, ev-44, ev-28].
- **Manual:** the EFDA template and calculator [ev-20, ev-22]; simply accepting defaults [ev-32, ev-17].
- **Service:** consultants [ev-01, ev-13, ev-14].
- **Founder-listed but not evidenced in this pack:** Sphera, Normative, Coolset and osapiens. A Coolset page was
  fetched but used only as background.

## Prospects (primary ICP: EU importers sponsoring Indian MSMEs)

There are 7 EU fastener importers and distributors: Fabory, Bossard, REYHER, Fastbolt, Arvid Nilsson, Heco
Nordiska and Würth Industrie Service. That is short of 20, because many distributor sites (Bufab, Etra, pgb,

## Method notes

- 14 queries, including contradiction searches on the 50-tonne exemption, defaults being enough, free
  support, and actual emissions exceeding defaults.
- Source mix: news, industry publications, company sites, one review and a directory. No job postings.
- Unfetchable and excluded: alcircle, Business Standard, Moneylife, Eurometal and SteelRadar. SteelRadar
  carried the Indian Steel Association's claim that actual emissions can exceed defaults.
- One analysis (SMM) gave far lower 2026 costs than GreenSutra's worked example. It was left out because the
  two conflict and GreenSutra shows its arithmetic.
- Several cost and impact figures come from CBAM vendors with a commercial interest (CleanCarbon.ai,
  GreenSutra).
