# Research report: Construction progress automation

- **Idea:** AI tool that automates construction progress reports from site photos and notes (US)
- **Preuve idea:** `9f259df8-99c5-4f88-b3b1-e1d57cecbb66`
- **Method:** `/research-idea` v1
- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Verification:** 22 of 22 quotes verified against the live pages by Preuve

## Bottom line

The reporting work is real and recurring: weekly owner reports and contractually required monthly
owner and lender reports [ev-01, ev-07, ev-08]. Data still moves between tools by hand at most
firms [ev-10, ev-13].

But the idea as stated, turning site photos and notes into reports with AI, **runs straight into
the market leader**. Procore (46.2% PM adoption in 2021 [ev-15]) already markets AI agents that
draft daily logs from field photos, notes and audio, and summarize logs for stakeholders
[ev-18, ev-19, ev-20]. Field capture is also already largely digital [ev-12].

**Evidence for the problem is moderate. Evidence that the obvious solution is already being built
by the incumbent is strong.** Don't build the generic version yet. A narrower wedge may exist
(see "Where an opening might be").

## What supports the problem

- **Weekly reports have a named owner and a hard deadline.** At Brinkmann, a general contractor,
  "the engineer is responsible for creating, formatting, and sending weekly reports no later than
  end of day each Friday", and managers review them before they go out [ev-01, ev-02]. The work
  sits with project engineers more than with PMs. That's a useful correction to the hypothesis.
- **Monthly reports are contractual.** Loan agreements can require a progress report to the
  lender's engineer "within five days after the end of each month" [ev-07], and monthly progress
  reporting is a standard clause [ev-08].
- **Data handling is still manual.** More than half of firms manually transfer data between
  unintegrated apps [ev-13]. In 2018, under a fifth consistently used apps beyond email, text and
  phone [ev-10]. Over two-fifths still run project management in spreadsheets [ev-14].
- **Photos are part of the work and easily lost.** Photo captioning is an explicit step in the
  weekly report [ev-04]. A vendor says phone photos often become disconnected from reports, and
  paper reports require re-entry in the office [ev-05, ev-06]. These two are vendor claims.

## What argues against it

- **The incumbent ships this.** Procore's Daily Log Agent will "Convert field photos, notes, and
  audio files into structured logs automatically" and "drafts your daily logs for final human
  verification" [ev-18, ev-19]. A starter agent "Extracts key progress updates from a daily log to
  create a concise, actionable summary" for stakeholders [ev-20].
- **Capture is already digital.** Daily reporting (62.4%) and photo/video capture (60.2%) are the
  top mobile uses in the field [ev-12].
- **Adjacent AI progress tools are established.** OpenSpace turns 360° walkthrough footage into a
  navigable record within a day [ev-17].
- **Budgets are thin.** Over a third of firms spend less than 1% of sales on IT [ev-16].
- **Reports are bespoke.** "Each owner could have their own set of requests or requirements"
  [ev-03], which limits one-size-fits-all automation.

## Unknowns

| Unknown | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| How PMs collect and format photos and notes into reports | Partially answered | Engineer drafts, manager reviews, formats vary by owner; no first-hand PM walkthrough yet |
| Time spent per week on reports | **No evidence found** | Only a general "14+ hours of non-optimal time" figure [ev-09]; reporting-specific numbers were vendor-only and unverifiable |
| Who holds purchasing authority | Partially answered | GTM guidance: PM champions, CFO is the economic buyer [ev-21, ev-22]; not survey-backed |
| Existing tools used | Answered | Procore dominant, with AI agents [ev-15, ev-18–20]; spreadsheets widespread [ev-14] |
| How often reports are required | Answered | Weekly to owners [ev-01]; monthly to owners and lenders [ev-07, ev-08] |

**New unknowns raised (added in Preuve):**

- How good are Procore's agents in practice?
- Do non-Procore general contractors feel enough pain to adopt a standalone tool?
- How long does one weekly or monthly report take?

## How it's solved today

Field teams capture on mobile [ev-12]. Project engineers compile weekly owner reports, which
managers review [ev-01, ev-02]. Many firms bridge tools by hand or with spreadsheets
[ev-13, ev-14]. Procore users now get AI-drafted logs and summaries inside the platform
[ev-18–20].

## Where an opening might be (hypotheses, not evidence)

- **Owner- and lender-specific formatting:** taking the data a firm already has and producing
  each owner's or lender's required format, monthly lender reports in particular [ev-03, ev-07].
- **The roughly half of GCs not on Procore,** who still use spreadsheets and email [ev-14, ev-10].
- Both need interviews with project engineers before any building.

## Method notes

- **Searches:** 10 queries (listed in the pack). Two were contradiction searches, on existing
  tool satisfaction and incumbent AI features.
- **Sources Preuve couldn't fetch (left out):** many vendor and review sites block crawlers via
  robots.txt or HTTP 403 (letsbuild, Sitemate, Capterra, TrustRadius, Software Advice, OpenSpace,
  Autodesk). As a result, reviews of current tools (e.g. Raken) aren't represented, which is a gap.
- **Source quality:** ev-05, ev-06 (Fieldwire), ev-18–20 (Procore) and ev-21, ev-22 (a marketing
  agency) are vendor or marketing sources. ev-09–11 come from a 2018 survey; ev-12–16 from a 2021
  survey.
- **Not used:** time-saving figures that appeared only in search snippets from vendor blogs.
