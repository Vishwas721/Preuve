# Vertical AI voice front desk for appointment-based local businesses: research report (2026-11-09)

Idea: `694e138d-ed84-4afe-84a6-06be0c0f17e0` · method v3 · pack `pack-2026-11-09.json`
Import: 93 verified, 0 unverifiable, 0 rejected. All six unknowns are *partially answered*. 2 new unknowns were added.

## Bottom line

The problem is real and widely described. Dental practices are commonly said to miss about 30–38% of calls, and callers who reach no one tend to try the next business. But almost all the numbers come from vendors, and the only independent dental study is from the UK in 2011. The bigger issue is the market, not the problem. US dental AI receptionists that book straight into Dentrix, Eaglesoft and Open Dental already sell at about $299–$349 a month, which is inside the idea's price range. Incumbents are also bundling the feature: Weave for dental, ServiceTitan for home services. In India, Hindi/Hinglish clinic voicebots already sell at ₹4–6 a minute. On this evidence, "pick one vertical and integrate deeply" is already crowded in US dental and home services. The open question is whether there is a gap these players leave, not whether the pain exists.

## What supports the problem

- **Miss rates are high (mostly vendor-sourced).** Benchmarks put dental practices at 30–38% of calls missed during business hours [ev-01]. A cited Peerlogic analysis of 4,280 calls across 26 practices found 38% unanswered [ev-15]. An independent 2011 UK dial-out study found 44% of calls failed to reach a person [ev-04]. CallRail puts health care at 32% [ev-05].
- **Callers move on.** In Invoca's 2026 survey (as cited), 66% of home-services buyers hung up when nobody answered, and 51% stopped after contacting one provider [ev-07]. Callers who can't get through tend to dial the next name on their list [ev-11]. One dentist's anecdote: new patients hit voicemail and booked the next clinic [ev-33].
- **The money at stake is plausible but modelled.** Estimates include $100k–$200k+ a year for a mid-size dental practice [ev-02], about $1,200 per missed home-services call [ev-09], and about $16k a month of exposure for a firm taking 1,000 calls a month [ev-08]. All of these are assumption-driven models. Vendor case studies report recovered bookings: 330 new-patient appointments in 12 months at a one-provider office [ev-23], and 21% more booked jobs at a ServiceTitan customer [ev-28].
- **Staffing is the root cause.** 62% of dentists named staffing shortages as their top challenge for 2025 [ev-30], and front-desk hires are hard to find [ev-31]. Extra staff and basic answering services give only marginal relief [ev-03]. At some prospects, assistants double as front desk [ev-56, ev-75].

## What argues against it

- **Incumbents bundle it.** Weave's AI receptionist is now available to all dental practices [ev-20, ev-25]. ServiceTitan's built-in voice agent books jobs after hours and "aims to reduce the need for third-party answering services" [ev-26]. ServiceTitan also argues that standalone agents lack the scheduling data its own agent has [ev-27]. That is the same "integration moat" the idea relies on, already held by the platform.
- **Vertical dental players already exist at the target price.** Dentina ($299/mo) and Viva AI ($349/mo) book directly into the major PMSs [ev-18]. Arini serves groups and DSOs [ev-22, ev-24]. Market price bands are $149–$239 for small practices and $300–$700 for 4–6 chair practices [ev-16].
- **The miss rate is lower than claimed for home services, and a missed call is not a lost job.** CallRail puts home-services missed calls at 14% [ev-05]. Spam, repeat callers, vendors and callers who accept a callback inflate the raw rate [ev-06].
- **Callers may not accept an AI.** In a survey commissioned by a human-answering firm, about a third of callers said they would hang up on an AI bot, and 85% prefer a human for local services [ev-12].
- **Self-scheduling is a substitute.** A claimed 80% of patients prefer practices with online scheduling, and 73% book after hours when they can [ev-32]. That covers part of the after-hours job with no phone call.
- **The horizontal layer is commoditised.** Agencies resell GoHighLevel voice stacks, and commenters say $1,000 a month is too much [ev-35]. Retell costs $0.07–$0.31 a minute [ev-29]. Another commenter calls a single dentist's loss "an anecdote, not a market" [ev-34].
- **India is not untouched.** Edesy books OPD appointments in Hindi [ev-39], and CallMissed handles Hinglish at ₹4–6 a minute [ev-38]. A starting receptionist earns about ₹17k–45k a month [ev-41], which caps what clinics will pay.
- **Prospect-level counter-signal.** One prospect, Cooper Owens DDS, already advertises "Phones Answered 24/7" [ev-59].

## How it's solved today

- **Direct:** Arini [ev-22, ev-24], Dentina [ev-18, ev-23] and Viva AI [ev-18] in US dental; Edesy [ev-39, ev-40] and CallMissed [ev-37, ev-38] in India.
- **Indirect:** Weave AI Receptionist, bundled with the dental phone platform [ev-19, ev-20, ev-25]. ServiceTitan's AI voice agent, bundled with the FSM [ev-26, ev-27, ev-28]. Retell as a horizontal building block [ev-29]. Online self-scheduling [ev-32].
- **Service:** Smith.ai, AI with human backup at $150/mo for 75 calls, or humans from $300/mo for 30 calls [ev-21, ev-42]. Swiss Monkey, a remote human front office at $399–$599/mo plus fees [ev-17].
- **Manual:** front-desk staff, often doubling as assistants, plus basic answering services [ev-03].
- **Founder-listed but not researched individually:** Hello22, LuMay, Vapi, Synthflow and Bland. This pack has no evidence on them.

## Method notes

- 22 search queries in all: problem, competitor, contradiction (AI rejection, self-scheduling, WhatsApp, incumbent bundling, price pressure) and prospecting.
- Source mix: industry publications (Dental Economics, ADA News, eesel review), vendor and company sites, two Reddit threads read through a public snapshot mirror (reddit.sentinel-team.org), a UK trade news item and a salary index.
- Most statistics are vendor-authored. The Dental Economics missed-call piece is written by an AI-phone vendor's developer, and the ServiceMag piece cites research commissioned by a human-answering company.
- Not citable (blocked or 404): Group Dentistry Now, Dentaltown, the Jobber community, Orrick's note on the FCC's 2024 ruling that AI voices fall under the TCPA (relevant to outbound reminder calls), ACHR News, AmbitionBox and Ruby. The FCC/TCPA consent risk is therefore not evidenced in this pack.
- India coverage is thin: 5 items, all vendor or salary data. There is no evidence on WhatsApp versus phone booking.
