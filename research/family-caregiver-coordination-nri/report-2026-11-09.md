# Family caregiver coordination OS (NRI angle): research report (2026-11-09)

Idea id: `cc53664f-b7db-4dc7-87c9-831404c482a6` · Method v3 · 61 evidence items, all verified (0 unverifiable, 0 rejected).

## Bottom line

The problem is real and well documented. NRI families coordinate parents' care through WhatsApp groups, a local relative or neighbour, and PDFs shared in chat, and sources describe this as fragmented. The weak point is a *software-only* product that families pay for themselves. In India, the families who pay buy human care-manager services, and those already bundle an app, a coordinator and records access (Emoha from Rs 1,999/month). In the US, coordination software is mostly paid for by employers (Wellthy, Cariloop, Bright Horizons), and those vendors already ship sibling care circles and medication trackers. The evidence supports the pain, but it argues against the planned $15-40/month direct-to-consumer software wedge. If anything here works, it is the concierge or service layer.

## What supports the problem

- NRI families coordinate through large WhatsApp groups where "information arrives in fragments" and relatives interpret updates differently [ev-30]. Medical reports arrive in the family group as PDFs [ev-31], and the standard hospitalisation advice is "set up a family WhatsApp group" with one local coordinator [ev-29].
- Parents of NRIs often face emergencies with only friends at their bedside, and friends are the first line of support [ev-10]. Time zones and parents hiding health concerns make remote oversight hard [ev-14].
- NRIs do pay. Anvayaa's early adopters were "almost entirely NRIs" [ev-20]. Attendants cost Rs 15,000-50,000 a month and are paid from NRO accounts [ev-12]. Apollo prices its NRI 360 plans in dollars, at $1,199-$2,499 [ev-33].
- US scale: 63M caregivers spend about $7,200 a year out of pocket [ev-01]. Employed caregivers report nearly $1,000 a month in caregiving costs [ev-16]. Coordination challenges are documented in a 2,000-caregiver survey [ev-28], and sibling conflict over appointments and aides is common [ev-17].
- The employer channel is real and growing. Wellthy's covered lives grew from 100k to 2M between 2019 and 2022 [ev-26]. 35% of Guardian's caregiving staff used its benefit [ev-43]. Mariner's advisers opened 300+ cases in two weeks [ev-56].

## What argues against it

- **Incumbents already bundle the software.** Emoha's entry plan includes a care coordinator, an app for adult children, and records and prescriptions access at Rs 1,999/month (about $23) [ev-07]. Anvayaa offers a care manager plus an app with real-time updates [ev-38]. Wellthy has Care Circles, a medication tracker and 190+ country availability [ev-27]. Cariloop covers task and medication coordination [ev-41]. MIT's Bright Horizons benefit includes a shared coordination hub [ev-42]. Lotsa Helping Hands is free [ev-40].
- **Direct-to-consumer payment has failed before.** Wellthy started direct-to-consumer, moved to employer-paid by 2017, and no longer promotes private pay [ev-25].
- **Adoption is low.** Only about 7% of caregivers used caregiving tech in AARP's study [ev-18], and only 9% use scheduling assistants (AARP 2026) [ev-19]. An operator says most elderly parents won't go beyond WhatsApp [ev-21].
- **The paying segment is narrower than it looks.** Families with a nearby sibling or a working local network "probably DON'T need a paid service" [ev-05].
- **The market is modest.** Anvayaa, a decade in, had FY25 revenue of Rs 14.6 crore (about $1.7M) [ev-23]. New entrants keep arriving: Nila raised a $2.4M pre-seed [ev-24], and ParentPulse is on a waitlist [ev-32].

## How it's solved today

- **Manual:** family WhatsApp group plus a local relative, neighbour or friend [ev-29][ev-30][ev-10].
- **Direct (India):** Emoha [ev-07], Anvayaa [ev-38][ev-20], Nila [ev-24], ParentPulse [ev-32].
- **Direct (US, employer-paid):** Wellthy [ev-27], Cariloop [ev-41], ianacare [ev-39]. Free option: Lotsa Helping Hands [ev-40].
- **Service:** Apollo HomeCare NRI 360 [ev-33], Healync [ev-09], Papa ($30/hour) [ev-34], Honor/Home Instead [ev-35].
- **Indirect:** Bright Horizons elder care benefits [ev-42][ev-51]. Khyaal, a senior lifestyle app rather than a coordination tool [ev-37]. CarePredict, which sells to senior-living operators, not families [ev-36].
- **Founder-listed with no public evidence found:** Hera (couldn't identify it), Caring Village (its site blocks Preuve's crawler), Samarth (its site blocks the crawler), and the CareZone shutdown (no fetchable source).

## Prospects (primary ICP: US working adults via employer caregiver benefits)

There are 16 employers and insurers whose own pages or news show they already buy a caregiving benefit: Dow, Medtronic, Guardian Life, City of Philadelphia, SMU, Stanford, Yale, Penn, UVA, MIT, University of Maryland, Best Buy, Mariner, Notre Dame, AT&T and Lafayette College. Three named contacts come from their own pages: Dow's CHRO, Medtronic's VP of Global Benefits, and Guardian's Head of Product and Digital Offerings. This is below the 20-company target. All 16 are already served by Wellthy, Cariloop or Bright Horizons, so they show who buys, not open demand. No headcounts were recorded.

- 25 searches, including contradiction searches on caregiver-tech adoption and app shutdowns. Every quote was copied from Preuve's fetcher and checked mechanically before import.
